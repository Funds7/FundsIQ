Free DELSU GST Study Guides

General Studies courses connect university learning with communication, critical thinking, civic knowledge, science, culture and practical life. This page introduces the GST courses currently organised on FundsIQ and explains the main areas students can revise in each course.

Use the topic lists to plan your reading. They are study outlines, not a replacement for your lecturer's notes, official course handbook or current examination instructions. Course coverage and terminology can vary by academic session.

How to study with these outlines: Read one topic at a time, write a short explanation in your own words, identify unfamiliar terms, and practise recalling the key ideas without looking at your notes. Confirm details and prescribed readings with your department.

First Semester

GST 101 — Use of English and Library

This course develops the communication and information skills students use throughout university. Effective study involves understanding how language works, expressing ideas clearly, reading critically and locating reliable academic sources.

Topics to revise

  • English lexis, word formation and sentence structure
  • Essay writing, paragraph development and punctuation
  • Phonetics and pronunciation
  • Reading, studying and public-speaking skills
  • Library services, reference sources and citation
  • Electronic libraries and copyright

GST 102 — Philosophy and Human Existence

Philosophy encourages careful examination of assumptions, arguments and questions about knowledge, reality, values and human society. Logic helps students distinguish supported conclusions from weak reasoning.

Topics to revise

  • Meaning, nature and branches of philosophy
  • Periods in Western philosophy
  • Major philosophical problems
  • Ethics and contemporary moral issues
  • African philosophy and national development
  • Democracy, human rights, logic and fallacies

GST 103 — DELSU Culture and Ethics

University orientation helps students understand the environment in which they study, the services available to them and the responsibilities that come with membership of an academic community.

Topics to revise

  • Delta State environment
  • History and organisation of Delta State University
  • University service departments and their functions
  • Campus safety and security
  • Student unionism and alumni relations
  • Library management, services and academic excellence

Second Semester

GST 111 — Nigerian Peoples, Culture and Entrepreneurial Skills

Studying people and culture involves examining shared practices, values, institutions and creative expression. Entrepreneurial topics connect skills and ideas with identifying needs and creating useful products or services.

Topics to revise

  • Nigerian peoples, cultures and historical change
  • Government, politics, norms and values
  • Music, arts and creative enterprise
  • Indigenous beliefs and cultural practices
  • Marketing concepts
  • Physical environment and disease prevention

GST 112 — History and Philosophy of Science and Technology

Science uses observation, evidence, testing and reasoned explanation to investigate the natural world. Technology applies knowledge and practical methods to solve problems, while scientific and technological choices can affect society and the environment.

Topics to revise

  • Evolution and humanity's place in the universe
  • History of science and scientific methodology
  • Science and technology in everyday life
  • Energy sources, generation and use
  • Pollution and environmental effects of chemicals
  • Living things, agriculture and pesticides

GST 113 — Peace Studies and Conflict Resolution

Peace studies examines why disagreements develop into conflict and how individuals, communities and institutions can prevent violence. Conflict resolution includes communication, negotiation, mediation and other approaches to addressing disputes.

Topics to revise

  • Concepts and theories of peace and conflict
  • Root causes of conflict in Africa
  • Sports and peace enhancement
  • Women and youth in peace-building
  • Alternative dispute resolution
  • Media, traditional institutions, the AU and UN

GST 114 — Communication in French

Introductory French develops the ability to understand and use common expressions in everyday situations. Regular practice with pronunciation, vocabulary, listening and short conversations helps learners build confidence.

Topics to revise

  • French alphabet and pronunciation
  • Introducing yourself and talking about family
  • Giving directions and stating nationality
  • Numbers and plural forms
  • Asking and telling the time
  • Useful travel, airport and occupation vocabulary

Build a consistent revision routine

Start with the course outline, then compare each topic with your lecture materials. Turn headings into questions, practise explaining answers aloud, and revisit difficult topics after a short break. For factual subjects, check dates, definitions and institutional details against authoritative course materials rather than relying on memory alone.

For language and communication courses, practise actively: write paragraphs, identify grammatical patterns, read aloud and use new vocabulary in sentences. For philosophy and peace studies, explain the reasoning behind an argument and consider a relevant example. For science topics, connect each concept to evidence, applications and possible effects on people or the environment.
